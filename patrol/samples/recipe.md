## where it's from, and why old kimchi

quick background since people ask: it's korean, kimchi-bokkeum-bap, and the [wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimchi_fried_rice) calls it a variety of bokkeum-bap (fried rice), made mainly with kimchi and rice plus whatever's around, "diced vegetables or meats like Spam." so the spam isn't me being lazy, it's on the record. the same page says "leftover chilled rice and over-ripened kimchi are usually preferred" and that they give a richer flavor and texture than fresh, which matches what my pan tells me every week. the rest of what i know about it is from eating it, mostly at a bunsik counter near my old apartment where it came with a small bowl of soup and cost less than the bus home. that's the version i'm chasing here, not a restaurant one.

## what you need (one plate, gym portion)

- day-old rice, about 1.5 cups (fresh rice works if you spread it on a plate for 10 minutes so the steam gets out)
- old kimchi, a big handful, roughly chopped. the sourer the better
- spam, half a can, diced (pork belly is the upgrade, chicken thigh works, so does firm tofu if you press it first)
- a spoon of kimchi juice from the jar
- gochujang, a teaspoon, optional. if your kimchi is properly sour you probably don't need it
- butter, a small pat, for the end
- 1 egg
- a little neutral oil for the pan

## the order, and where the heat goes

1. rice first if it's fresh. dump it on a plate, spread it thin, leave it while you chop. cold fridge rice skips this. i keep a container from the day before in the fridge basically for this reason.
2. pan on high. actual high, not medium-high. a little oil, then the spam. let it sit until it gets some brown on it, maybe 2 minutes, and don't stir it constantly, you want the cubes to get crispy on a side.
3. kimchi in, straight into the spam fat. this is the step i'd tell you to slow down on. leave it alone. let the edges go dark and a little sticky on the pan. it takes longer than you think, 3 or 4 minutes. it smells kind of harsh at first and then it turns sweet, and when it turns sweet you move on.

4. rice in. break it up with the spatula, press it flat against the pan, leave it 30 seconds, flip, press again. you want the grains actually hitting the metal, not riding on top of the kimchi.
5. the spoon of kimchi juice goes in now, around the edge of the pan and not on top of the rice. gochujang too if you're using it. stir it through so the color is even.
6. off the heat, butter in, stir until it's gone. i started doing this because the bunsik place version had a richness i couldn't get with oil alone, and butter was the first thing i tried that got close. i still don't know if that's what they used.
7. fry the egg in the same pan after you plate the rice (or a second pan if you're fancy). runny yolk. break it over the rice with the spoon and mix as you go.

## the mistake, which is usually two mistakes

wet fresh rice and too little heat. both at once, usually. the rice is still steaming from the cooker, the pan is on 6 out of 10 because you're scared of the nonstick, and what comes out is kimchi rice porridge. tastes fine i guess but it steams instead of fries and you lose all the dark crispy bits, which for me are the reason to make this at all. dry rice and a hot pan fix most of it, and then you have to stop touching it, which is the harder part. my pan is scratched to hell from doing this most weeknights and i keep meaning to replace it, but the scratched one is the one i know, so it's still the one that comes out of the cupboard.

## time and cost

15 minutes if the rice is already cold. closer to 25 if you're drying fresh rice on a plate.

cost is about 3 dollars a plate the way i do it. spam is the expensive part, half a can runs around 1.50 at my store. rice and kimchi are almost nothing per plate if you buy the big jar, and the egg is whatever eggs cost this week.

for the gym people since someone always asks: it's mostly carbs and spam fat, i'd guess somewhere around 700 calories the way it's pictured, i haven't weighed it. i add a second egg on push days when i need the protein. it's a "just lifted, want food in 15 minutes" kind of meal, so don't build a cut around it.
