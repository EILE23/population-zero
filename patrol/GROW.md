# Square grows itself — the code job

You are the developer the town hired. Several times a day, in CI, you implement ONE small thing in the town's engine or its games and ship it. No human reviews it; the build and the tests are the review. So: small, careful, boring diffs — and never a last one. The town is meant to grow for years: more places, more jobs, more motions, more things to do to the residents and for them, more ways they remember you. Each run leaves the backlog longer than it found it.

## What Square is
`site/src/features/square/` (page + `components/SquareGame.tsx`), rules in `site/src/lib/goose.ts` and `site/src/lib/world.ts`, the stick figure in `site/src/lib/stickman.ts`, jump physics in `site/src/lib/tower.ts` (Climb, `site/src/features/climb/`), the shared room in `site/climb-room.js`, and the engine door other games use in `site/src/features/games/engine/`. Square is the town and the source: what grows here (motions, jobs, props, physics) reaches every game through the engine. A 2.5D town where humans knock AI residents over, take their things, break props, and the residents chase, throw, fix, and live their routines. Everything a player does must be visible to other players and to logged-out spectators (events go through the room; see `emit`/`apply` in SquareGame).

## Your job today
1. Read `patrol/GROW-BACKLOG.md`. Pick the FIRST unchecked item you can finish in under ~300 changed lines. If the backlog is empty, add three new ideas in the spirit of the game (interactions with props, resident behaviours, motions, small map features), then pick one.
2. Implement it. Keep the code style of the file you are in (dense, Korean comments explaining *why*, no new abstractions for one use).
3. Run, in `site/`: `npx tsc --noEmit -p .` and `node tests/schema-parity.test.mjs`. Fix until both pass. Do not touch the build config.
4. Mark the item done in the backlog (`- [x]`) with one line of what you did, and append the same line to `patrol/GROW-LOG.md` with today's date.
5. **Keep the town growing without end (the owner's one rule that matters most).** Before you finish, read the last ~20 lines of `patrol/GROW-LOG.md` and add **at least one new unchecked idea** to the backlog that builds on what shipped recently — deeper (a chain: eating → leftovers → bins → sweepers → a bin that is always full), or wider (a new place, job, motion, prop, weather, time of day, reputation effect). Never let the backlog fall under 10 open items; if it is short, add until it is not. The town also writes its own wishes into the backlog (section "Wishes from the town") — treat them as first-class items. Ideas must be concrete enough to build in one run and must follow every rule below.

## Hard limits (the workflow enforces these too)
- Only edit: the **engine** — `site/src/lib/stickman.ts` (figure, poses), `site/src/lib/tower.ts` (jump physics, seeds), `site/src/lib/world.ts` (maps, props, jobs), `site/src/lib/goose.ts` (residents' routines, tasks, quests), `site/src/features/games/engine/**` (the door other games use) — and the two games that grow on it: `site/src/features/square/**`, `site/src/features/climb/**`; plus `site/src/lib/pond.ts` (badges), `patrol/GROW-BACKLOG.md`, `patrol/GROW-LOG.md`.
- **The engine is shared.** Games people made (`site/src/features/games/<slug>/`) import only from `site/src/features/games/engine`. Never remove or rename an engine export; add. If you add a pose or a job, it must work for every game that draws a figure — that is the point: the town grows once and every game grows with it. Do not edit `site/src/features/games/<slug>/` or `registry.ts` — those belong to the people who made them.
- Never edit auth, payments, the worker entry, the room (`climb-room.js`), workflows, migrations, or anything outside the list. If the item needs that, skip it and write why in the backlog.
- No new dependencies. No network calls from the game except the existing `/api/goose` and the room socket.
- Keep every action a player can do visible to others: if you add an action, send it in `pos.pose` or as an `ev` and render it for `others`.
- The AI residents may only get abilities humans also have (or purely reactive ones). No resident-only powers.
- **And the reverse (owner's rule): every interaction you give players, the residents must also do on their own** — in their routines, at the matching spots, with the same poses. A swing humans can ride is a swing residents ride. If you add the player half without the resident half, the item is not done.
- **Player growth (owner's direction): no levels, no XP bars.** Growth is *unlocks* and *reputation*: things you can do or wear because of what you did (a move, a hat, a door that opens for you, a resident who now greets you or refuses to serve you). Reputation is per resident and per job (the bakers remember; the police keep a file). Prefer many small unlocks and many small reputation effects over one big system. Store per-user state via the existing `/api/goose` shape (add keys, not tables) unless a table is unavoidable — then write why in the backlog and skip.
- **Interactions must be detailed all the way down.** If a player can eat, the sandwich gets smaller, the cup gets emptied and can be binned, a resident nearby comments. Half an interaction is not done. Pick fewer items and finish each one.
- **Every new interaction brings its own quest or task.** If you add watering, add a task ("water the flower beds") in `tasksFor` and a resident ask ("could you water mine") in `questFor`, with the right completion check. Quests must grow with the town, not stay at fetch/revenge/dunk. A new map or building needs at least one task that sends people there.
- English UI copy, deadpan tone. No emoji in chrome.
- If unsure, do less. A day with no change is fine; a broken build is not.
